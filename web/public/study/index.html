For every school, we compared the ARP's ratings with those of every other rater who saw the same school in the same quarter. We measured this against DIET mentors and SRGs, who sit at zero (the dashed line).

Generous raters also stop telling classrooms apart

Up the side is answer variety: how often an ARP's answer to each FLN question changes from one classroom to the next. At 0, every classroom gets the same answers. At 1, answers split 50/50.

The two go together. The more generous an ARP, the more their forms are the same "yes" on every visit.

Four rating styles

  • All-clear . Near-identical "yes" forms, and 30% or more of their spot assessments report every child Saksham.
  • Box-ticking . Near-identical "yes" forms (answer variety below 0.35).
  • In between . Everyone else: answers vary only somewhat, or vary but score more than 5 points above DIET/SRG.
  • Discerning . Answers vary (0.5 or more) and they rate no more than 5 points above DIET/SRG.

What this is, and isn't

This is a picture of how forms are filled in. It doesn't measure how well an ARP coaches. A box-ticking ARP may still be a good mentor, and a discerning one may not be.

But it shows the forms can't be read at face value. A district dashboard that adds up box-ticked forms will show progress that outside observers don't see. Look up any ARP below.

How this was measured

Data

Classroom observations from the mentor app in 10 districts (), as used by the ARP visit dashboard. Spot assessments come from the mentor spot-assessment workbooks. Names, districts and blocks are as recorded in the app.

Classroom score

The share of practices marked as observed on a form: 13 questions on the Grade 1–3 FLN forms, 9 on the Grade 4–8 form. On the Grade 4–8 form, "no lesson plan" skips the rest, so those forms are left out of the score and counted separately.

Generosity

A rater-effects model. Each form's score is treated as the school's level that quarter, on that form type, plus the rater's own lean. It is fitted over every school-quarter seen by two or more raters (ARPs, DIET mentors or SRGs), and scaled so DIET mentors and SRGs average zero. Split-half reliability is r = .

Answer variety

For each of the 13 FLN questions, 4 × p × (1 − p), where p is the share of an ARP's forms answering "yes"; averaged across questions. 0 means the ARP gives the same answer every time; 1 means an even split. Split-half reliability is 0.92.

Styles

Applied in order. All-clear: variety below 0.35, and 30% or more of spot-assessment school-months report every child Saksham. Box-ticking: variety below 0.35. Discerning: variety 0.5 or more and generosity of +5 points or less. In between: everyone else. The thresholds sit at natural breaks in the data. ARPs with fewer than 60 visits or fewer than 10 shared-school comparisons are listed as "too little data".

Limits

DIET mentors and SRGs are a yardstick, not a gold standard. They are self-reporting too and may be stricter than the truth. Ratings in the same quarter may come from different days or classrooms. None of this measures coaching quality or learning gains. The data is rebuilt automatically as new monthly exports arrive.
