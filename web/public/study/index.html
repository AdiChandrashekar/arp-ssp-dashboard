Breaking down supportive supervision habits

ARPs fill in a classroom observation form on every school visit. Read answer by answer, each ARP's forms follow habits that hold month after month: how often they mark a practice as seen, whether they use the middle answers such as "some" and "partial", how often they end the Grade 4–8 form at its first question, and what they write down for attendance.

Answer variety

For each question, answer variety measures how often an ARP's answer changes from one classroom to the next: 0 if it never changes, 1 if it splits evenly. An ARP's score is the average over the 12 questions.

An ARP's score from odd months is almost the same as their score from even months (r = ), so variety is a lasting habit. The grade observed has little effect: FLN answers in Grades 1, 2 and 3 differ by 1 to 3 points.

Three recording styles

  • Uniform , variety below 0.35. Their FLN forms are close to identical, and on average of them give the top answer to every question.
  • Mixed , variety from 0.35 to 0.65.
  • Varied , variety of 0.65 or more. Their answers change from classroom to classroom, and of their FLN forms give the top answer throughout.

Some answers rule out later ones. Teachers use the weekly assessment tracker to update the NIPUN Talika and to form Group A for remedial work, so a teacher who isn't filling in the tracker can't have done either.

What the forms can and can't tell us

These measures describe how ARPs fill in the form. Coaching quality and children's learning are outside what this data can show: a Uniform ARP may coach well, and a Varied ARP's answers may be partly noise.

How this was measured

Data

Every classroom observation in the mentor app exports for the 10 programme districts, from . Names, districts and blocks are as recorded in the app. Each ARP is identified by a hashed phone number.

Grades

Comparisons are made within a grade. Positivity compares each answer with the average on the same form, grade and month; revisit checks compare the same school, form and grade. FLN answers differ by 1 to 3 points between Grades 1, 2 and 3 (see the FLN page), so the mix of grades an ARP observes has little effect on their style.

Answer variety

For each of 12 FLN questions, 4 × p × (1 − p), where p is the ARP's average answer (the top answer scores 1, middle answers between 0 and 1), averaged across the questions. Split-half reliability, odd against even months: r = .

Styles

Uniform: variety below 0.35. Mixed: 0.35 to 0.65. Varied: 0.65 or more. A k-means clustering of all the stable measures found the same main split. It also grouped together ARPs who mark every teacher present; that turned out to depend on their schools as much as on the ARP, so it appears as a marker on profiles.

Contradictions

Four checks on FLN forms: the teacher doesn't have the Sandarshika and is recorded as referring to it; the tracker isn't filled in and the NIPUN Talika is recorded as updated; the tracker isn't filled in and remedial work with Group A is recorded as done; no child did last week's worksheets and all are recorded as checked.

Limits

The measures describe form filling only, and say nothing about coaching or learning. Some school facts do change between visits. An ARP's schools shape some numbers, attendance most of all. The pages rebuild automatically when new monthly exports arrive.
